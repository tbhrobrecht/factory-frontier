# Factory Frontier: game guide and legend

Use this guide while running `python -m student.student_view` from the repository
root. The [workspace README](README.md) covers setup and student files; the
[walkthrough](WALKTHROUGH.md) explains how to get started with your agent.

## What is the factory trying to do?

Your agent controls a **moving factory** and chooses one action per turn.
Coordinates are `(row, column)`, with `(0, 0)` at the top-left corner.

- **Single-player presets:** start at the bottom-left and reach the top-right
  cell, `(0, width - 1)`, before the step limit. Reaching it wins immediately.
  Losing the factory to danger ends the episode in defeat. Running out of
  time truncates the episode without a win.
- **Competition:** player 0 starts at the bottom-left and player 1 at the
  top-right. Keep your factory alive as danger closes in. If only one factory
  survives, its owner wins; if both are lost together, the result is a draw.
  Reaching the opposite corner does not trigger a win in this mode.
  At turn 60, if both survive, compare `(alive, collected_resources, energy)`
  in that order. More collected resources beats any energy difference. Equal
  scores produce a draw. This round deadline is termination, not truncation.

Resources help you earn reward and replenish energy. Movement, jumps, and
building can spend energy, so choose routes and builds that support the goal.
Zero energy does not itself destroy the factory, but it can prevent actions.

The navigation/refuelling presets are `model_free_control`,
`function_approximation`, `policy_methods`, `ppo` and `competition`. Other
built-in presets reward each step with `-0.01`, each gathered resource unit with
`+1`, a win with `+10`, and a loss with `-10`. These rewards guide learning; they
are separate from the competition score above. Custom configurations can change
the weights.

Those five presets use `-0.1` per step and `+0.05` per resource, with the same
win/loss rewards. In single-player, mining earns net `-0.05`. In competition,
mining supplies energy only, giving net `-0.1`; only finite collectibles count
toward resource points. Losing your factory in competition also costs `-10`,
including on a simultaneous-death draw. Refuel when needed rather than mining
for points.

## Printed map legend

These are the symbols shown by `env.render()` and `student.student_view`.

| Symbol | Meaning | Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| `F` / `f` | Factory | The unit controlled by its owner's actions. Moves, jumps, builds, collects resources, and uses `MINE`. Losing it determines survival. |
| `S` / `s` | Scout | A buildable, stationary unit. Can collect a resource on its cell, but currently does not move or increase visibility. |
| `W` / `w` | Worker | A buildable, stationary unit. Can collect a resource on its cell, but currently does not automatically build, move, or transport resources. |
| `M` / `m` | Miner | A buildable, stationary unit. Can collect a resource on its cell, but currently does not automatically mine. `MINE` acts through the factory. |
| `#` | Wall | Blocks movement into its cell. Can be generated by the map or built and removed by the factory when those actions are enabled. |
| `*` | Collectible resource | Collected once when a living entity occupies its cell. Adds to its owner's gathered total; collection by a factory also adds the same amount of energy. |
| `n` | Renewable mining node | `MINE` adds one energy; it also adds one gathered resource in single-player. Competition mining gives no resource points. The node is not consumed. |
| `.` | Empty ground | A cell with no currently displayed entity, wall, resource, or node. |

Uppercase always means **player 0** and lowercase **player 1** in the printed
map. In single-player, your factory is `F`. Units occupy cells and block factory
movement into those cells. There are no attack actions in the current engine.

An entity's symbol hides the terrain or resource beneath it. A resource also
hides a mining node until collected, so a cell may change from `*` to `F` and
later to `n`. The goal has no special symbol: find it by its coordinates.
Destroyed entities disappear from the map.

## Danger and visibility

**Danger has no ASCII symbol.** A printed `.` can still be in a dangerous
column. Read `observation["danger"]` to track the boundary.

When enabled, danger advances every `boundary_interval` turns (six in
`policy_methods`, eight in `ppo` and `competition`, five in the other built-in
presets). The boundary starts at
column `-1`; its first advance covers column `0`. At the end of every turn,
living entities in covered columns are destroyed, including those entering an
already-covered column. Walls do not
protect them. Danger is resolved before goal detection.

- In single-player, covered columns satisfy `column <= danger_column`:
  danger moves from left to right.
- In competition, it advances from both sides: columns satisfying
  `column <= danger_column` or `column >= width - 1 - danger_column` are covered.

The four danger features are: enabled flag, boundary column divided by map
width, distance from your factory to its side's boundary divided by width, and
`boundary_interval / 100`. Destruction is checked every turn;
the printed map does not mark covered cells between checks.

In partially observable presets, the agent sees only cells within Manhattan
distance `visibility_radius` of its factory. Scouts do not extend that range,
and walls do not block this visibility calculation. The ASCII renderer shows
the full map for debugging; the agent's observation can contain less information.

## Numeric observation legend

`observation["grid"]` uses numbers rather than printed characters. Friendly
and opponent codes are relative to the agent receiving the observation.

| Grid code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `0` | Empty, unseen, or padding: check `visibility` and `map_shape` to distinguish them. |
| `1` | Wall |
| `2` | Resource |
| `3` | Mining node |
| `5`, `6`, `7`, `8` | Friendly factory, scout, worker, miner respectively |
| `9`, `10`, `11`, `12` | Opponent factory, scout, worker, miner respectively |

There is no grid code for danger or the goal. The grid is padded to `(16, 16)`;
`map_shape` gives the active `(height, width)`. Entity codes cover underlying
terrain. Use `entity_mask` to identify populated rows in the `entities` array.
The separate `(16, 16)` binary `mining_nodes` layer identifies visible mining
nodes even when a resource or factory covers their grid symbol. Unseen nodes
are zero in partially observable presets.

## Actions and building

Always choose an action whose entry in `observation["action_mask"]` is `1`.
Actions unavailable in the current preset or state are masked out. An invalid
action resolves to `WAIT` and still uses a turn.

| Action index | Action | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| `0` | `WAIT` | Stay in place; time and cooldowns still advance. Does not regenerate energy. |
| `1`-`4` | `NORTH`, `SOUTH`, `EAST`, `WEST` | Move the factory one cell in that direction. Some presets can randomly change the direction (slip). |
| `5`-`8` | `JUMP_NORTH`, `JUMP_SOUTH`, `JUMP_EAST`, `JUMP_WEST` | Move two cells; the landing cell must be free and in bounds. Can jump over an occupied cell or wall. |
| `9` | `BUILD_SCOUT` | Build a scout next to the factory; costs 4 energy. |
| `10` | `BUILD_WORKER` | Build a worker next to the factory; costs 5 energy. |
| `11` | `BUILD_MINER` | Build a miner next to the factory; costs 6 energy. |
| `12` | `BUILD_WALL` | Build an adjacent wall; costs 2 energy. |
| `13` | `REMOVE_WALL` | Remove an adjacent wall for no energy cost. |
| `14` | `MINE` | Gain one energy on a mining node; also gather one resource in single-player, but no resource points in competition. |

Builds choose the first free adjacent cell in this order: **east, south, west,
north**. Wall removal uses the same order among adjacent walls. You do not
select a direction for these actions. A build starts a cooldown: with the
default configuration, two intervening turns must pass before another build.
Movement and other legal actions remain available during that cooldown.

In `model_free_control`, factories start with 8 energy and movement costs 1.
In `function_approximation` and `policy_methods`, they start with 16 energy,
movement costs 1, and jumps cost 2. In `ppo`, factories start with 20 energy,
movement costs 1, and jumps cost 2. In `competition`, factories start with 16
energy, movement costs 1, and jumps cost 2. Early presets start with 20 energy
and have free ordinary movement.
These costs and build settings can be overridden in custom configurations.

The default `model_free_control` map guarantees a mining node at `(7, 3)`,
reachable in three east moves, and a wall-free route onward to the goal. Five
collectibles and random walls vary by seed. At least one mining action is
required: starting energy plus all five collectibles supplies only 13 energy,
while reaching the goal requires at least 14 moves. Danger covers the entire
map at step 40, matching the episode cap; win before then. Death at the deadline
is a termination rather than a time-limit truncation.

`function_approximation` uses the same safe-refuelling generator on a 12 by 12
map, with a mining node at `(11, 3)`, 14 collectibles, and a 60-step deadline.
Moves, jumps, waiting and mining are available. Building and wall removal are
disabled; jumps can cross walls but must land in a free cell. The default map
has a solvable route even without jumping, letting you compare strategies and
feature representations on the same seeded layouts.

`policy_methods` keeps those layouts, fuel costs and actions, and adds 8% slip
to ordinary movement. The intended direction occurs with 92% probability; each
other cardinal direction occurs with `8% / 3`. A blocked realized move stays
in place. Jumps, waiting and mining do not slip. Danger advances every six turns,
covering the map at the 72-step deadline. The map has a feasible route, but bad
slips can still cause a loss. Use a stochastic policy to experiment with policy
gradients or actor-critic methods; the agent's sampling and the environment's
slips use separate random generators.

`ppo` uses four-room 14 by 14 layouts with doorways, local observations within
Manhattan distance 3, 20 collectibles, and a reachable mining node at `(13, 3)`.
Doorways and a route to the goal are protected from random obstacles. Ordinary
moves have 5% slip; jumps, mining and waiting do not slip. Danger advances every
eight turns and covers the map at the 112-step deadline. Navigation, jumps and
mining are enabled; building and wall removal are unavailable. Learn from the
masked observation, optionally with memory, rather than reading the full debug
map. The PPO algorithm itself is a student exercise.

`competition` uses a rotationally mirrored 16 by 16 map with 24 finite
collectibles and paired mining nodes. The two central columns are clear, and
protected escape lanes connect both spawns to them. Partial visibility has
Manhattan radius 4. Moves have 5% slip; jumps do not slip. At turn 60, columns
7 and 8 remain safe and the resource/energy score decides between survivors.
There is no combat, building or wall removal. Each player chooses its action
before the turn resolves; attempts to enter the same empty cell both fail,
costing time but no energy. Occupied starting cells cannot be entered, even if
the other factory moves away. The complete match log and final score are in
`MatchResult`; per-player `info` includes only that player's events.

## Which mechanics appear in each preset?

| Preset | What to focus on |
|---|---|
| `intro` | Deterministic movement on a 5 by 5 map; reach the top-right goal. |
| `mdp`, `dynamic_programming` | Same goal, with stochastic movement and an exposed transition model. |
| `monte_carlo_td` | A 6 by 6 map with stochastic movement and no exposed model. |
| `model_free_control` | Random walls, resources, mining, energy costs, and advancing danger; builds and jumps are unavailable. |
| `function_approximation` | Larger solvable map, meaningful fuel costs, and jumps; building and wall removal are unavailable. |
| `policy_methods` | Stochastic navigation and refuelling, jumps, and a 72-step deadline; building and wall removal are unavailable. |
| `ppo` | Room maps, doorways, fuel, jumps, stochastic movement, and local observations; building and wall removal are unavailable. |
| `competition` | Fair mirrored resource/survival contest, partial observations, simultaneous turns and terminal scoring at turn 60. |

For example, run `python -m student.student_view --config function_approximation`
to explore the navigation, refuelling and jumping task. The starter agent chooses
random legal actions, so it may need several runs or a better policy to demonstrate
every mechanic.
